# Experiment 0b: Early Cheetah CIR Pilot

**Status:** Three exploratory variants completed
**Task:** DMC Cheetah Run with vision observations
**Environmental change:** Action gain `1.0 -> 0.7`
**Platform:** CPU
**Runs:** Critic-first, actor-only, and world-model-only
**Dates:** 20--21 July 2026
**Evidence level:** Mechanistic pilot; not yet a seeded paper result

## Executive summary

This experiment was the first end-to-end test of Continual Imagined Rehearsal
(CIR). It began from a competent Cheetah Run checkpoint, reduced every actuator
command to 70% of its original magnitude, repaired the world model from real
replay, and compared three choices for behavioural adaptation:

1. train the critic and then the actor with persistent current-posterior IR;
2. freeze the critic and train only the actor with persistent IR; and
3. freeze both actor and critic and train only the world model.

The main result is simpler than the original design anticipated:

> The strongest reliable adaptation signal appeared before either the actor or
> critic was updated. Across all three independent executions of the same
> protocol, 128 world-model-only updates raised the first B evaluation from a
> shock mean of 278.0 to approximately 414.9.

Persistent critic rehearsal then caused a catastrophic behavioural collapse.
Freezing the critic prevented that collapse, but persistent actor rehearsal
still accumulated policy drift and did not improve on the policy seen directly
after model repair. The world-model-only run was at least as good as the
actor-only run on the ten B collection episodes, retained strong predictive
diagnostics on both A and B, and ended with the best B milestone score. However,
episode returns were highly variable and milestone evaluations contained only
one unseeded episode per condition. The last observation is therefore promising,
not conclusive.

The current best working belief is:

- the gain change directly invalidates the learned action-to-next-state mapping,
  so the world model is the component that definitely requires adaptation;
- the existing state-value critic can remain useful because the task and reward
  semantics are unchanged and desirable latent states are still desirable;
- the actor may eventually need some adaptation to recover the strongest gait,
  but the always-on, constant-rate persistent actor IR used here is too aggressive;
- the critic should remain frozen unless diagnostics show that its state-value
  ordering has actually become wrong; and
- the next experiment should first evaluate the existing source and final
  world-model-only checkpoints with controlled repeated episodes and a diagnostic
  that can see behavioural drift caused by changing latent representations.

This conclusion does **not** diminish IR's intended role in CIR. It tells us
that behavioural rehearsal should be invoked in response to demonstrated
behavioural error, rather than assumed necessary after every dynamics change.
It also supplies the first concrete evidence for independently gated predictive
and behavioural learning.

## Relationship to the preceding experiment

The preceding [frozen-world-model critic diagnosis](EXPERIMENT_0A_CRITIC_DIAGNOSIS.md)
was deliberately artificial. It zero-masked 20% of a trained critic, held the
actor and world model fixed, and asked whether the standard bootstrapped critic
objective could reconstruct the damaged value function. The corruption was
extreme in function space: it nearly destroyed value ordering and introduced
large outliers. Repeated critic-only IR diverged at every tested horizon even
though on-dataset world-model reward and return predictions remained excellent
through horizon 48.

That experiment established two useful facts:

- long imagined horizons were much more trustworthy than the original horizon
  of 6 might have suggested, at least on trajectories generated by the healthy
  actor; and
- the existing self-bootstrapped critic objective is unsafe when its starting
  critic is severely damaged.

It did **not** establish that a naturally stale critic in continual learning
would be damaged enough to require repair. Experiment 0b therefore removed
artificial corruption and introduced a real dynamics change. The critic
bootstrap alternatives remain a valuable fallback, but this pilot asks the more
important prior question: which behavioural component actually needs updating?

## Concepts and terminology

### Imagined Rehearsal

Imagined Rehearsal (IR) starts from the agent's current posterior belief,
samples multiple plausible latent states, rolls those states forward through
the learned world model, and uses the imagined outcomes to update behaviour.
Unlike ordinary replay-based Dreamer training, the behavioural update is tied
to the state the agent currently occupies rather than to arbitrary sequences
sampled from replay.

### Monte Carlo Posterior Batching

Monte Carlo Posterior Batching (MCPB) takes multiple stochastic samples from
the current posterior. These samples share the current deterministic recurrent
state but represent uncertainty in the stochastic latent state. Each sample is
then imagined forward. In this pilot, every rehearsal trigger used 128 samples.

### Continual Imagined Rehearsal

CIR separates two learning pathways:

```text
real replay sequences  ---> predictive world-model learning

current posterior      ---> imagined trajectories ---> behavioural learning
```

The encoder, recurrent dynamics, decoder, reward head, and continuation head
are trained only from real stored experience. Actor and critic learning, when
enabled, occurs only through current-posterior imagination. This experiment is
the first practical test of that separation; the broader programme is described
in the [CIR research plan](CIR_RESEARCH_PLAN.md).

## What a 30% reduction in actuator strength means

### Exact numerical transformation

The policy continues to produce a continuous action vector. The normal DMC
action wrapper first limits each scalar component to the legal interval
`[-1, 1]`. Task A passes that clipped command through unchanged. Task B then
multiplies it by `0.7` immediately before the environment step:

```text
u_A = clip(u_policy, -1, 1)
u_B = 0.7 * clip(u_policy, -1, 1)
```

This is a **30% reduction**, not a 70% reduction. The environment receives 70%
of the usual command. It is multiplication by `0.7`; it is not subtraction of
`0.7`.

| Raw policy output | Clipped A command | B command after `0.7` gain |
|---:|---:|---:|
| `0.0` | `0.0` | `0.0` |
| `0.2` | `0.2` | `0.14` |
| `0.5` | `0.5` | `0.35` |
| `0.8` | `0.8` | `0.56` |
| `1.0` | `1.0` | `0.70` |
| `1.4` | `1.0` | `0.70` |
| `-0.5` | `-0.5` | `-0.35` |
| `-2.0` | `-1.0` | `-0.70` |

For example, a policy vector

```text
[1.0, -0.5, 0.2, -1.4]
```

becomes

```text
A: [1.00, -0.50, 0.20, -1.00]
B: [0.70, -0.35, 0.14, -0.70]
```

Each actuator is scaled independently. The direction and relative pattern of
the commands are preserved, but all commands have less control authority.

### Why the actor cannot simply command more

About 52% of the scalar action components in these runs were already close to
saturation under the logged threshold `abs(action) >= 0.95`. To turn a desired
environment command of `1.0` into `1.0` after a `0.7` multiplier, the policy
would need to request approximately

```text
1.0 / 0.7 = 1.43.
```

That request is clipped to `1.0` before scaling, so the environment still sees
only `0.7`. The actor cannot recover full control authority merely by increasing
the magnitude of already saturated outputs. It would need to discover a gait
that uses weaker actuation more efficiently: different timing, coordination,
posture, or momentum.

### What changes physically and what does not

The intervention approximates weaker actuators or a global reduction in torque
authority. It does not imply that speed, return, acceleration, or distance will
fall by exactly 30%. Cheetah physics are nonlinear; contacts, balance, joint
limits, momentum, and the learned gait interact over time.

The observation format, termination rules, task definition, and reward function
are unchanged. The principal changed relationship is:

```text
(latent state, commanded action) -> next latent state
```

This makes the intervention primarily a world-model dynamics problem. The
critic is a state-value function, approximately `V(z, h)`, rather than an
action-value function `Q(s, a)`. A running-like latent state can therefore remain
valuable even though the action needed to reach it has changed. The actor may
need to relearn how to reach valuable states, but the critic does not
automatically need to relearn which states are valuable.

### What is stored in replay

Replay stores the agent's commanded action, before the hidden environment gain
is applied. The environment internally applies the `0.7` multiplier. This is
intentional: the world model observes that the same commanded action now leads
to a different next observation and must learn the altered transition mapping.

The RSSM also applies its existing internal action normalization. Commands
outside the legal interval have already been clipped by the environment wrapper,
so this remains consistent with the original DreamerV3 action path. The action
gain does not mutate the policy's action array in place, and the relevant unit
test explicitly checks this.

### A subtle identifiability issue

The replay contains both A and B experience, but the agent receives no explicit
regime identifier. The same observed state and commanded action may therefore
have two different next-state mappings. Recurrent history can potentially infer
the regime after observing its consequences, but at the first step of an episode
the regime is not identifiable from the supplied input.

This pilot is consequently a small non-stationary partially observable problem,
not a clean task-conditioned problem. That is scientifically interesting, but
it also means mixed replay can force one model to average incompatible dynamics,
especially when short sampled windows begin with fresh recurrent state.

## Research questions

The three completed variants address the following sequence of questions:

1. Does the 30% action-gain change produce a clear but recoverable performance
   shock?
2. Can predictive world-model learning improve behaviour before any actor or
   critic update?
3. Does critic-first actor-critic IR support continuing adaptation under the
   repaired model?
4. If critic updates are harmful, can a frozen competent critic guide actor-only
   IR?
5. If actor-only IR also fails to help, is world-model adaptation alone sufficient
   for this change?
6. Which diagnostics distinguish predictive failure, critic failure, policy
   drift, distribution shift, and ordinary return variance?

The intended outcome was not necessarily success. The pilot was instrumented
to expose the first failure strongly enough to choose a precise next experiment.

## Common experimental protocol

### Source agent and environment

All variants loaded:

```text
checkpoints/cheetah_run_vision_seed0
```

The task was `dmc_cheetah_run` under the vision configuration. Task A used
action scale `1.0`; task B used action scale `0.7`. Every episode contained
1,001 recorded observations.

### Phase schedule

Each run followed the same high-level schedule:

1. collect three frozen episodes on A;
2. collect two frozen shock episodes on B;
3. perform 128 model-only replay updates;
4. evaluate once on B and once on A with all learning disabled;
5. collect ten further B episodes;
6. perform 32 model-only replay updates after every collected B episode; and
7. evaluate once on B and A after B collection episodes 1, 3, 5, and 10.

The initial and per-episode updates produced

```text
128 + 10 * 32 = 448 world-model updates.
```

Official evaluations did not enter replay and never performed adaptation. At
the end of a run, replay contained 3 A episodes and 12 B episodes: the two
initial shock episodes plus ten collection episodes.

### World-model learning

World-model batches contained eight sequences of length 32. `replay_context`
was set to zero, so independent sampled windows began from a fresh recurrent
carry rather than from cached latent context produced by an older encoder/RSSM.
Replay remained the ordinary FIFO store.

Only the following namespaces received model-only gradients:

```text
enc/  dyn/  dec/  rew/  con/
```

The update used a fresh optimizer with a constant learning rate of `4e-5`.
World-model updates occurred only between episodes; the recurrent carry was
never made stale by changing the encoder or RSSM halfway through a live episode.

### Behavioural rehearsal when enabled

Rehearsal started at the first live posterior and then every 25 environment
steps. Every trigger used:

```text
MCPB posterior samples: 128
imagination horizon:  12
optimizer steps:      1 per enabled component
learning rate:        1e-4, constant
```

The critic-first variant performed a critic-only warm-up at the first trigger
after initial model repair. Later triggers updated the critic, recomputed the
relevant behavioural quantities, and then updated the actor. The actor-only
variant used the same actor rollout and optimizer but never updated the critic.
The world-model-only control did not call the rehearsal path.

### Completed variants

| Variant | World model | Actor | Critic | Behavioural purpose |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Critic-first | 448 updates | 400 updates | 401 updates | Original CIR pilot |
| Actor-only | 448 updates | 400 updates | Frozen | Test competent critic as stable task knowledge |
| World-model-only | 448 updates | Frozen | Frozen | Isolate predictive adaptation |

The one extra critic update in the first condition is the post-repair warm-up.
The critic-first and actor-only runs imagined 615,936 and 614,400 transitions,
respectively. The world-model-only run imagined none for learning.

## Diagnostics and their interpretation

### Real episode return

Return is the end-to-end behavioural outcome. It includes environment initial
state, sampled RSSM state, sampled policy action, accumulated physical effects,
and any changes to the learned model or behaviour. It is the final criterion,
but a single episode is noisy and does not identify which component failed.

### Open-loop reward and return fidelity

Recorded real actions were replayed from posterior anchors through the model at
horizons 6, 12, 24, and 48. Predicted rewards and discounted returns were
compared with the subsequent real trajectory. Low error indicates that the
model can reproduce recorded data from those anchors.

This remains an on-trajectory diagnostic. A model can predict a failed gait
accurately without knowing how to return to a successful gait. Accuracy on the
actor's induced distribution is not the same as useful counterfactual coverage.

### Prior-posterior KL and deterministic-state drift

Prior-posterior KL measures disagreement between what the dynamics predicted
and what the encoder inferred after seeing the next observation. Deterministic
relative L2 measures how far an open-loop recurrent state moves from the state
reconstructed from reality. Both normally grow with horizon. They are useful
model-trust signals, but neither alone determines behavioural quality.

### Critic value versus realised H48 return

The probe correlates a state's critic prediction with the realised discounted
return over the following 48 recorded steps. Correlation measures ranking, not
absolute calibration. The critic predicts a much longer effective future than
48 steps, so the value MAE can be large even when ranking is excellent.

### Actor KL from the initial actor

The pilot evaluates the initial and current actor heads on the same **current**
latent features and computes their raw pre-clipping Normal KL. This detects
changes to actor parameters. It does not detect a behavioural change caused
solely by the encoder or RSSM mapping the same observation history to a different
latent feature. In the world-model-only run the actor KL is exactly zero by
construction, yet the complete observation-to-action function can still change.

The KL also operates before environmental clipping. With more than half of
action components close to saturation, a large raw distribution change may be
partly hidden by clipping, and a small raw change can still affect contact timing.

### Critic bootstrap diagnostics

For critic rehearsal, the code decomposes the imagined target into predicted
reward-only and bootstrapped contributions. The ratio answers whether the target
is primarily grounded in model-predicted rewards or in the critic's own value
estimate. A large ratio indicates a highly self-referential update.

### Namespace hashes and counters

Actor, critic, and model namespaces were hashed to detect unintended parameter
changes. Optimizer steps, model batches, rehearsal triggers, imagined
transitions, episode events, and probe events were counted independently. These
checks are essential because a plausible score curve cannot establish that an
ablation actually froze its intended component.

## Variant 1: critic-first actor-critic CIR

### Design

This was the planned first CIR implementation. After model repair, every
eligible live posterior produced a critic update and then an actor update. The
first trigger performed an additional critic-only warm-up. Both components used
persistent parameters: improvements or errors accumulated across episodes.

### Behavioural outcomes

The ten B collection returns were:

```text
301.14, 246.87, 30.97, 52.90, 6.63,
4.37, 3.61, 5.22, 3.64, 4.12
```

| Statistic | Critic-first B collections |
|---|---:|
| Mean | 65.95 |
| Sample standard deviation | 111.57 |
| Median | 5.93 |
| Minimum | 3.61 |
| Maximum | 301.14 |

The matched milestone evaluations show the timing of collapse:

| Milestone | B evaluation | A evaluation |
|---:|---:|---:|
| After initial model repair | 416.55 | 700.85 |
| After B episode 1 | 323.81 | 707.68 |
| After B episode 3 | 26.72 | 67.54 |
| After B episode 5 | 6.45 | 26.47 |
| After B episode 10 | 2.24 | 3.13 |

The fall to near-zero return is far outside ordinary Cheetah episode variance.
This variant failed decisively.

### What happened to the critic

On the actor's final live trajectories, critic-versus-real-H48 correlation was
approximately `-0.018` on B and `0.072` on A. Values on failed trajectories
became almost constant at roughly 218 while predicted rewards were close to
zero. The critic therefore stopped distinguishing useful from useless states on
the distribution that the changing actor actually visited.

At the same time, fixed-trajectory probes told a more nuanced story. On the
original competent B trajectory, value correlation remained approximately
`0.78--0.82`; on the fixed A trajectory, the final correlation was about
`0.987`. Thus the critic had not become uniformly random everywhere. It retained
ordering on familiar competent data while becoming uninformative on the
actor-induced failed manifold.

The critic target decomposition explained why repair was unsafe. The
bootstrapped contribution became hundreds of thousands of times larger than
the immediately predicted reward contribution in late failed episodes. The
update was therefore driven overwhelmingly by the critic's own recursively
changing estimate, not by a strong independent reward teaching signal.

### What happened to the actor and state distribution

The final actor KL from its initial head reached approximately `42.68` on live B
and `63.71` on live A. Its fixed-trajectory KL was smaller but still substantial:
approximately `16.50` on B and `4.94` on A. Actor drift and critic error formed a
feedback loop:

```text
critic changes target
        |
        v
actor follows changed critic
        |
        v
visited state distribution deteriorates
        |
        v
critic receives local rehearsal on poorer states
```

The world model could accurately predict portions of this low-reward
distribution, but accuracy there did not provide examples of how to return to a
successful running manifold. Current-posterior IR is deliberately local. Once
the live posterior had moved into nearly stationary behaviour, local imagined
rollouts mostly described variations of failure.

### Interpretation

This run reproduced the central warning from Experiment 0a in a less artificial
setting. Critic-first ordering is not enough if the critic target is dominated by
a moving bootstrap. Updating the actor after the critic made the critic's error
behaviourally consequential and caused the data distribution itself to collapse.

This does not prove that critic IR is impossible. Reward-only returns, fixed
target critics, trust-weighted bootstraps, longer horizons, or conservative
updates remain available. It does show that none should be introduced until a
continual change actually demonstrates that critic value ordering needs repair.

## Variant 2: actor-only IR with a frozen critic

### Design

The second run removed all critic learning. The online critic, slow critic,
return normalizers, and critic optimizer were frozen and checked by namespace
hash. Actor rehearsal retained the same MCPB count, horizon, trigger rate,
rollout, learning rate, and persistent optimizer as the critic-first run. This
was a single-factor behavioural ablation relative to the first run.

### Behavioural outcomes

The ten B collection returns were:

```text
133.64, 341.60, 326.43, 67.73, 188.57,
343.79, 185.29, 283.58, 138.93, 169.91
```

| Statistic | Actor-only B collections |
|---|---:|
| Mean | 217.95 |
| Sample standard deviation | 98.52 |
| Median | 186.93 |
| Minimum | 67.73 |
| Maximum | 343.79 |

The run no longer collapsed to zero, confirming that critic learning was the
major cause of the catastrophic first result. It nevertheless failed to improve
on the policy evaluated immediately after model repair:

| Milestone | B evaluation | A evaluation |
|---:|---:|---:|
| After initial model repair | 407.95 | 651.47 |
| After B episode 1 | 404.26 | 694.13 |
| After B episode 3 | 252.60 | 769.97 |
| After B episode 5 | 118.72 | 509.12 |
| After B episode 10 | 201.56 | 215.67 |

The final live critic correlations remained strong: approximately `0.904` on B
and `0.835` on A. Live model H12/H48 return correlations were also high at
approximately `0.993/0.990` on B and `0.991/0.982` on A. There was no evidence
that an exploding critic or globally unusable model forced the decline.

### Actor drift

Actor KL grew as persistent updates accumulated:

| Milestone | Live B actor KL | Live A actor KL |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1.133 | 1.080 |
| 3 | 3.157 | 2.052 |
| 5 | 7.771 | 4.279 |
| 10 | 6.275 | 9.059 |

Across these four milestone points, the descriptive correlation between score
and KL was about `-0.960` on B and `-0.969` on A. With only four points, these
are not inferential statistics. They are nevertheless consistent with harmful
cumulative actor drift.

The actor's mean imagined advantage was slightly negative in each collection
episode, generally around `-0.003` to `-0.012`. The optimizer continued to
apply approximately 40 updates per episode regardless of whether the marginal
update improved real behaviour. There was no actor trust region, source-policy
anchor, slow actor, adaptation gate, or stopping criterion.

### Why this is not a direct test of original IR

The actor-only variant should not be described as “original IR failed.” The
original stationary IR mechanism used the legacy optimizer schedule and local
episodic adaptation semantics. This CIR control used:

- a dedicated constant-rate `1e-4` optimizer;
- no optimizer warm-up;
- approximately 400 persistent updates;
- MCPB-128 at every trigger; and
- no mechanism to discard or roll back a harmful local update.

It is a clean ablation against the critic-first CIR branch, not a faithful
replication of the original IR evaluation protocol.

### Interpretation

A frozen state-value critic was indeed much more stable than a continually
updated one. However, the actor did not require hundreds of unconditional
updates simply because dynamics had changed. The post-repair policy was already
substantially better on B. Persistent local optimization gradually moved it
away from the source policy without robust evidence that each move improved the
real task.

If actor IR is reintroduced, it should initially be conservative: lower or
legacy learning rate, fewer updates, an explicit policy-KL limit, comparison to
a frozen source actor, and a gate based on measured marginal benefit. A
one-episode local adapter that can be discarded is also scientifically distinct
from permanently changing the base actor.

three-A/two-B shock design, the same 128-update initial model repair, the same

This variant isolates the effect of updating the predictive representation and
dynamics while preserving the learned actor and value heads.

### Frozen weights do not mean a frozen complete policy

The actor namespace hash was exactly unchanged after every model block, and
the actor-head KL was exactly zero. That proves that the actor parameters did
not move. It does **not** prove that the complete observation-to-action function
was unchanged:

```text
observation history
      -> encoder/RSSM latent       (updated)
      -> frozen actor head         (unchanged)
      -> action
```

Changing the encoder or RSSM can move an observation history to a different
latent input to the same frozen head. The existing KL diagnostic evaluates the
old and current head on the same current latent and is blind to this
representation-induced behaviour change. This is the most important missing
diagnostic in the world-model-only control.

### Interpretation

The world-model-only variant is the most promising of the three, but its claim
must remain narrow:

- initial predictive repair repeatedly improved B before behavioural training;
- continued model-only learning did not visibly destroy fixed A or B predictive
  fidelity;
- a frozen critic continued to rank returns well; and
- unconditional actor IR did not outperform freezing the actor.

The data do not yet show that model-only learning reliably improves B across
time or retains A behaviour. Ten collection episodes are noisy and the milestone
evaluations are single samples. Controlled repeated evaluation is required
before choosing model-only CIR as the main algorithmic result.

## World-model optimizer behaviour

All three variants exhibited similar initial model-learning curves. The total
model loss was approximately 35--38 after the first 32 updates, about 9.7--10.6
after update 128, and approximately 7.0, 8.5, and 9.0 at the end of the
critic-first, actor-only, and world-model-only runs. Reward loss fell from about
3 initially to roughly 0.67--0.70 by update 128 and approximately 0.38--0.63 by
the end.

The fresh optimizer's update RMS was about `1.7e-5` in the first block and later
about `7e-6`. No non-finite loss or update was observed. These values confirm
that the model was learning, but optimizer loss cannot by itself distinguish
useful dynamics adaptation from representation drift or mixed-regime averaging.

## Integrated mechanistic interpretation

### 1. The world model is the directly invalidated component

The intervention changes what a command does, not what outcome the task rewards.
The source model initially overpredicted B rewards and showed higher latent
surprise. Model-only replay reduced both errors, and B score improved in all
three executions before any behavioural update. This is currently the most
direct causal evidence in the study.

### 2. The state-value critic was more transferable than expected

Because the critic receives latent state rather than the current action, it can
retain the concept that forward-running states are valuable while the dynamics
and actor relearn how to reach them. Strong live and fixed value correlations in
the actor-only and world-model-only runs support this intuition.

This is not a universal argument for freezing critics. A reward-function change,
new termination semantics, severe representation incompatibility, or entry into
genuinely novel state regions can invalidate `V(z, h)`. The result is instead a
case for diagnostic gating: train the critic when its ranking or calibration is
shown to fail, not merely because the environment changed.

### 3. Critic self-bootstrapping turned local error into policy collapse

The critic-first run updated a moving value function using targets dominated by
that same value function. The actor then optimized against it, changed the live
distribution, and deprived local rehearsal of competent-state coverage. The
collapse was a coupled actor-critic-distribution failure, not simply a bad model
prediction.

### 4. Persistent actor IR over-adapted

Freezing the critic removed the catastrophic feedback loop, but 400 always-on
actor updates still did not outperform no actor update. The inverse association
between policy KL and milestone score, combined with stable critic and model
diagnostics, makes cumulative actor drift the leading explanation.

The experiment does not show that actor adaptation is unnecessary forever.
Under a 30% control-authority loss, the source actor cannot restore saturated
commands and an improved gait should theoretically exist. It shows that the
present local, persistent, constant-rate rehearsal schedule does not find that
gait reliably enough to justify its compute or forgetting risk.

### 5. Model accuracy on a failed distribution is insufficient for recovery

High reward/return correlation in the critic-first failure might seem
contradictory. It is not. Once the actor visits mostly poor states, an accurate
model can faithfully predict poor outcomes. Current-posterior IR begins in that
local region and may not imagine far enough, diversely enough, or optimistically
enough to find the competent manifold. Model fidelity and behavioural coverage
are different requirements.

### 6. Horizon 12 was reasonable but not exonerated

Experiment 0a showed excellent on-dataset fidelity through horizon 48, making
horizon 12 a defensible compromise between reward propagation and effort.
However, those probes used recorded actions and posterior anchors. They do not
guarantee fidelity under a drifting actor or coverage from a poor live state.

Longer horizons may expose a route back toward reward, but they also compound
off-distribution dynamics error and increase compute. Horizon should eventually
be conditioned on model trust and behavioural coverage, not simply maximized.
The completed results do not justify a broad horizon ablation before the
evaluation and representation-drift confounds are repaired.

## What can and cannot be claimed

### Supported observations

- Multiplying actions by `0.7` created a substantial, nontrivial B shock.
- The initial 128 model-only updates reduced measured model error and were
  followed by a higher B score in all three runs.
- The implemented critic-first behavioural loop collapsed Cheetah behaviour.
- Freezing the critic prevented near-zero collapse.
- Actor-only persistent IR did not outperform the world-model-only control on
  mean B collection return.
- The frozen critic retained strong return ordering in the actor-only and
  world-model-only runs.
- The world-model-only run retained strong fixed-trajectory predictive metrics
  on both A and B.
- Existing actor-head KL cannot detect behaviour drift induced by updated latent
  representations.

### Not yet supported

- That world-model-only learning reliably solves the action-gain change.
- That A behaviour is retained after 448 world-model updates.
- That the final world-model-only B score of 452.09 represents a stable gain.
- That actors never require adaptation after a dynamics change.
- That critics generally remain valid across continual tasks.
- That the selected replay balance or update count is optimal.
- That the critic bootstrap is the only possible cause of critic-first failure.
- That the three runs form statistically matched learning curves.

## Code and artifact integrity audit

The implementation was reviewed against the original DreamerV3 and IR paths,
the saved configurations, the episode artifacts, the event streams, and the
runtime invariance checks. The executed runs are internally consistent with the
intended design. It is not possible to literally guarantee arbitrary scientific
software, but no defect was found that invalidates the conclusions above.

### Checks that passed

- The action gain is applied to a copy immediately before the DMC step and does
  not mutate the actor output.
- The stored replay action is the commanded pre-gain action, as intended.
- The episode/probe action and reward indexing is consistent; no open-loop
  off-by-one error was found.
- Model-only learning selects exactly the predictive namespaces and does not
  send gradients into actor or critic parameters.
- Actor-only learning leaves critic, slow critic, and value-normalizer state
  unchanged under the implemented namespace checks.
- World-model-only learning leaves actor and critic network namespaces unchanged.
- Model updates occur between episodes rather than inside a recurrent carry.
- The first critic-first trigger performs the intended warm-up, producing 401
  critic versus 400 actor updates.
- Run counters, model blocks, episode labels, and probe counts match the planned
  schedule.
- No duplicate completed episode or probe records were found.
- The original DreamerV3 joint-training loss was not changed by the CIR runner.
- The legacy actor-IR path retained its original semantics.
- Nine CIR-specific unit tests, five manual pure-function checks, and Python
  compilation checks passed during implementation/audit.

### Suspicious or limited implementation details

These issues did not invalidate the uninterrupted completed runs, but should be
addressed or explicitly controlled before a larger study.

1. **Environment randomness is not controlled by the run seed.** The DMC
   construction does not currently pass a task random seed. `--seed` controls
   JAX randomness but not environment initialization. Consequently the planned
   “paired seeds” evaluation is not genuinely paired.

2. **Evaluation remains stochastic.** The original agent ignores the textual
   `mode='eval'` distinction for actor and RSSM sampling. A frozen evaluation
   means no gradients, not deterministic action or latent inference.

3. **The source-checkpoint regex in the evaluation-suite configuration is not
   consumed by `eval_only`.** The current evaluation entry point uses its
   explicit checkpoint path. Future automation should avoid assuming that the
   regex changes the loaded agent.

4. **The model optimizer is fresh.** It resets Adam state and uses constant
   `4e-5` without the source optimizer's warm-up/history. This is intentional
   for isolation, but it is not identical to continuing the original Dreamer
   optimizer.

5. **Resume seed accounting can inherit ordinary update count.** On load,
   `n_batches` is reconstructed using the maximum of general and model-only
   update counters. A checkpoint created by ordinary training may therefore
   start the model-only RNG schedule at a large index. This does not affect the
   within-run update counts or the uninterrupted results reported here.

6. **Artifact writes and checkpoint writes are not one transaction.** A crash
   between the two could duplicate a final artifact when resuming. No duplicates
   were present in these completed runs.

7. **The critic freeze hash is network-focused.** The runtime hash covers the
   relevant critic networks but is less comprehensive for scalar auxiliary state
   such as `slowval_count`. The executed counter checks and code path provide
   supporting evidence, but a future hash should cover the complete critic
   learning state.

8. **Mixed-regime replay has no context label.** With `replay_context: 0`, a
   sampled window starts with fresh carry and may not identify whether its
   transition came from A or B. This is an experimental limitation rather than
   a coding bug.

9. **Checkpoint storage is large.** New optimizer states make completed CIR
   checkpoints roughly 3.47 GB each. This affects practicality and should be
   considered before seed sweeps.

10. **The full upstream test suite was not available.** The environment lacked
    some optional testing dependencies, including `pytest`/`zerofun`, so the
    targeted tests and compilation checks do not constitute exhaustive upstream
    regression coverage.

## Threats to validity

### Single stochastic milestone episodes

The largest limitation is evaluation noise. Milestone conclusions currently
depend on one episode per task. Cheetah Run has a substantial lower tail even
under the untouched source policy. Repeated evaluation is required.

### No truly paired environment seeds

The three variants did not experience identical initial physics states. Their
online trajectories also diverged as soon as their behaviour differed, so their
replay datasets ceased to be matched. They are valid comparisons of complete
online algorithms, but weaker causal comparisons of a single update rule.

### Different initial fixed trajectories

Each run's first A and B episodes became its fixed diagnostic trajectories. In
the actor-only run those happened to include unusually weak returns. Fixed
probe differences across runs therefore partly reflect different anchor quality.
A shared representative trajectory panel is needed.

### On-policy diagnostic coverage

Model probes follow recorded action sequences. They establish prediction quality
where the run actually went, not across the counterfactual state-action regions
needed to discover a better weak-actuator gait.

### No explicit regime identity

The model is asked to represent two dynamics mappings without being told which
one is active. This may be a desirable continual-learning challenge, but it
confounds retention capacity with context inference.

### Frozen heads over moving representations

Namespace freezing is parameter-level isolation, not function-level isolation.
Changing the encoder/RSSM can change both actor and critic outputs for the same
raw observation history even while their head weights remain identical.

## Current conclusions and research decisions

1. **Freeze the critic by default for the next experiment.** Its value ordering
   remains useful under this dynamics-only shift, while its current rehearsal
   objective is hazardous.

2. **Do not run always-on actor IR in the next experiment.** The first priority
   is to determine whether the model-only result is real. Actor rehearsal should
   later be added only when a diagnostic shows a residual behavioural need.

3. **Keep critic bootstrap alternatives documented, not active.** Reward-only,
   fixed-target, or trust-weighted returns are rational backups if a future task
   actually invalidates critic values.

4. **Treat world-model repair as the main signal from Experiment 0b.** It is the
   only intervention whose immediate benefit repeated consistently across all
   three runs.

5. **Measure functional representation drift.** Zero actor-head KL does not mean
   that the frozen policy behaves identically after the model changes.

6. **Do not infer forgetting from one A episode.** A retention claim requires a
   controlled evaluation distribution.

7. **Do not expand to a large benchmark yet.** One carefully controlled
   evaluation of the existing checkpoints is more informative than another
   expensive online run under the current measurement confounds.

## Smallest decisive next experiment

The next experiment should evaluate, not retrain, the existing source checkpoint
and final world-model-only checkpoint.

### Conditions

Evaluate both checkpoints on both regimes:

| Checkpoint | A scale 1.0 | B scale 0.7 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Original source | Required | Required |
| Final world-model-only | Required | Required |

Use at least 10 episodes per cell, preferably 20 if CPU time is acceptable.
Pass explicit deterministic DMC task seeds and reuse the same seed list in every
cell. Also control or record JAX keys used for posterior and action sampling.
Report mean, sample standard deviation, median, confidence interval, and paired
per-seed differences.

### Add an end-to-end behaviour-drift probe

For a fixed panel of raw A and B observation histories, run two complete paths:

```text
source encoder/RSSM -> source actor
current encoder/RSSM -> frozen actor
```

Compare:

- posterior latent displacement;
- action mean and standard-deviation displacement;
- pre-clipping policy KL;
- clipped action difference;
- action-saturation pattern; and
- critic prediction under source versus current latent features.

This probe directly measures the effect hidden by the current zero actor-head
KL.

### Use a shared fixed trajectory panel

The panel should contain several representative high-, middle-, and low-return
A and B trajectories. Every checkpoint should be probed on the same raw
histories. This removes the accidental dependence on each run's first episode.

### Decision rule

- If the final model-only checkpoint reliably improves B and retains A, make
  world-model-only adaptation the default CIR path and keep behavioural IR gated
  off until a measured residual justifies it.
- If B improves but A behaviour falls while fixed predictive fidelity remains
  healthy, investigate representation compatibility: latent anchoring, a slow
  target encoder/RSSM, distillation, or restricting which model modules adapt.
- If B does not improve reliably despite improved predictive calibration, add
  conservative actor adaptation: legacy-rate or lower-rate IR, few updates,
  source-policy KL control, and rollback/gating.
- Only adapt the critic if controlled probes show that its ranking or calibration
  genuinely fails. At that point compare reward-only, fixed-target, and standard
  bootstrapped critic objectives.

## Final research position

Experiment 0b began with the assumption that a changed environment would make
the critic stale, and that critic-first IR would repair it before the actor
learned. The evidence changed that position.

For this dynamics-only intervention, the critic appears closer to reusable task
knowledge than to a component requiring rapid continual learning. The actor may
need eventual improvement because weaker actuators cap its original saturated
commands, but aggressive persistent IR is not automatically beneficial. The
world model is the one component whose learned relationship was directly made
wrong, and repairing it produced the most repeatable immediate gain.

The emerging CIR design is therefore not “always train model, critic, and actor
in sequence.” It is:

```text
detect which learned relationship became wrong
                |
                v
repair the world model when prediction changed
                |
                v
measure whether value ordering or behaviour remains inadequate
                |
                v
invoke conservative critic or actor rehearsal only when justified
```

That is a stronger and more compute-conscious hypothesis than the original
fixed update schedule. The next controlled evaluation will determine whether
it is also supported by repeatable behavioural evidence.
